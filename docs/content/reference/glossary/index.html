The terms these docs use, in one line each. Where the DSL has a way to write the idea, the last column says how.

Rhythm (Tala)

TermMeaningIn the DSL
TalaThe rhythmic cycle a composition is set in, kept with claps, finger counts and waves\cycle("|4|2|2|")
AvartanamOne full round of the talaOne cycle; the renderer ends it with a double bar
AngaA part of the tala: laghu, drutam or anudrutamOne bar of the cycle
LaghuAn anga of a clap and finger counts; its length depends on the jatiA bar of 3, 4, 5, 7 or 9 beats
DrutamAn anga of 2 beats: a clap and a waveA bar of 2 beats
AnudrutamAn anga of 1 beat: a single clapA bar of 1 beat
JatiThe length of the laghu: tisra 3, chatusra 4, khanda 5, misra 7, sankeerna 9The laghu's number in \cycle
Sapta talasThe seven basic talas: Dhruva, Matya, Rupaka, Jhampa, Triputa, Ata, EkaSee Cycles
Adi talaTriputa in chatusra jati: 4 + 2 + 2 = 8 beats\cycle("|4|2|2|")
ChapuA family of talas counted without the laghu-drutam structure, such as Misra Chapu (3 + 2 + 2) and Khanda Chapu (2 + 3)\cycle("|3|2|2|"), \cycle("|2|3|")
Gati (nadai)How each beat divides: tisram 3, chatusram 4, khandam 5, misram 7, sankeernam 9\beatDuration(3) and so on
KalaiHow many counts each beat of the tala gets while keeping time; in 2-kalai Adi each beat is counted twiceNot a command; write the counts as beats, or fit more notes in each beat
Speed (kalam)Notes per beat. Second speed doubles the first, and third doubles it againDouble \beatDuration for each speed
SamamThe first beat of the avartanamThe start of the cycle
EduppuThe point in the tala where a line of the composition begins; it can come after samamFill the beats before it with _
KarvaiA unit where the previous note or syllable is held,
KorvaiA pattern played three times that ends on a set point of the talaLines, labelled with \line

Melody

TermMeaningIn the DSL
SwaraA note: Sa, Ri, Ga, Ma, Pa, Dha, NiS R G M P D N
SthayiOctave: lower (mandra), middle (madhya), upper (tara)Dots: .S lower, S. upper
SwarasthanaWhich variant of a swara a raga uses, such as suddha or prati madhyamamOften letter case, by the notation's convention
RagaA melodic framework: its swaras, their order up and down, and their characteristic phrases and gamakasFront matter, e.g. ragam: "Mohanam"
Arohanam, avarohanamA raga's ascending and descending scalesFront matter
SahityaThe words of a compositionA role line, Sh:
JantaiThe same swara twice in a row, as in S S R RTwo notes

Gamakas

A gamaka is an ornament on a note. In the DSL each one is a prefix, written directly before its note. The Embellishments reference has the full list of symbols.

TermMeaningIn the DSL

NokkuA stress on a note, reached from the note above it~w G
RaaviA struck emphasis on a note~^ G
KandippuA sharp, cut-off touch on a note~./ G
OdukkalOn the veena, deflecting the string to reach the next note without a fresh pluck~x G
